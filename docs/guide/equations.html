      Equations

      Polynomial equations, systems and inequalities solved completely, and certified roots of other equations in an interval.

      Before I solve anything, I ask myself whether I need every solution or one root in a particular interval. For a polynomial equation or system, SymLean gives every real solution, and the theorem says that the printed list holds all of them, so nothing is missing. That completeness is the important part. Rational solutions are exact. An irrational one is a closed form such as 2 in solve, or it is named by an interval that holds exactly one solution. An inequality is reduced to a union of intervals. And for an equation with other functions, such as cosx=x, I ask for a certified root in an interval.

      Exact solutions

      • solveEvery real solution of a polynomial equation or system, with a theorem that the list is complete.
      • reduceA polynomial equation or inequality reduced to an equivalent union of intervals, with a theorem.

      Roots

      • rootsEvery real or complex root of a polynomial equation, exact or enclosed, with a theorem that the list is complete.
      • findRootA certified simple root of a function in an interval, to a chosen number of decimals, with a uniqueness theorem.

      Newton showed his method on this cubic. It has one real solution, and the theorem says that there are no others. The decimal is truncated to 20 places, and the theorem puts the root in a rational interval of width 10−20:

      No algebra solves this next equation, so I ask for a root in [0,1]. The theorem says that exactly one solution lies within 10−10 of the printed value:

      #cas [findRoot x in [0, 1]] (cos x = x)

      The computation

      • Certified root

        Sign change found by interval sampling and bisection (not proof)
Unique root in [0.7390851331, 0.7390851333], within 10^-10 of 0.7390851332: endpoint signs and a derivative of one sign, checked by the kernel

      The exact modes stop at polynomials. solve and roots take polynomial equations with rational coefficients, reduce takes polynomial relations and quotients compared with 0, and for other equations findRoot is the mode. A nonlinear system goes through a Gröbner basis and needs finitely many complex solutions, so a circle on its own is refused. findRoot finds one simple root at a time. It samples the function at 65 points of the interval and narrows the first sign change from the left, and then the kernel checks opposite signs at the ends of a small interval and a derivative of one sign on it. That’s also where it stops. It refuses a double root, or any root where the derivative vanishes, two roots closer than the sample spacing, and a sign change across a pole. For a polynomial with a multiple root, roots is the mode to use.
