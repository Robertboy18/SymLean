      Number theory

      Primes, factorizations, divisors, congruences, digits and continued fractions, each with a theorem about Mathlib’s definitions.

      In number theory the answers quickly get too big to check by hand, and that’s when I’m most tempted to trust a computer algebra system blindly. Here you don’t have to. You can work with primes and factorizations, divisors, congruences, digits, continued fractions and the usual combinatorial numbers, and each answer is a theorem about Mathlib’s definitions, such as Nat.divisors, Nat.ModEq, Nat.digits or Nat.primeFactorsList. Its proof checks the answer instead of trusting the search that found it. Primality and integer factorization come from the optional Hex library, which has the kernel replay a primality certificate for every prime.

      Primes and factorizations

      • primeQWhether a natural number is prime, with a proof of Nat.Prime n or its negation, through the optional Hex library.
      • factorIntegerThe prime factorization of a natural number n≥2 with a theorem about Nat.primeFactorsList, through the optional Hex library.
      • moebiusMuThe Möbius function μ(n) of a positive integer from its prime factorization, through the optional Hex library.

      Divisors and arithmetic functions

      • divisorsThe divisors of a positive integer below 1012 in increasing order, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.divisors.
      • divisorSigmaThe sum σk(n) of the k-th powers of the divisors of n, with a theorem about Mathlib’s ArithmeticFunction.sigma.
      • eulerPhiEuler’s totient φ(n), the number of residues modulo n coprime to n, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.totient.

      Modular arithmetic

      • powerModThe remainder of ab modulo m by repeated squaring, for exponents with hundreds of digits, with a theorem in ℕ.
      • modularInverseThe inverse of an integer modulo m≥2 in ZMod m, or a proof that there is none.
      • chineseRemainderAll solutions of a system of congruences x≡ri(modmi), as one residue modulo the lcm, or a proof that there are none.
      • extendedGCDThe gcd of two natural numbers with Bézout coefficients s and t, and a theorem that as+bt=gcd(a,b).
      • jacobiSymbolThe Jacobi symbol (a/n) of an integer and an odd natural number, with a theorem about Mathlib’s jacobiSym.

      Digits and continued fractions

      • integerDigitsThe digits of a natural number in any base b≥2, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.digits.
      • continuedFractionThe continued fraction [a0;a1,…,ak] of a rational number, with a theorem that it equals the number.
      • fromContinuedFractionThe rational value of a finite continued fraction [a0;a1,…,ak], with a theorem in ℚ.

      Combinatorial numbers

      • binomialThe binomial coefficient (nk) of natural numbers n and k, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.choose.
      • factorialThe factorial n! of a natural number, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.factorial.
      • fibonacciThe Fibonacci number Fn, with F0=0 and F1=1, and a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.fib.

      The Sunzi Suanjing asks for a number with the remainders 2, 3 and 2 on division by 3, 5 and 7. The answer is complete. The theorem says more than that 23 works: the solutions are exactly the numbers 23+105k:

      In[1]:=
      #cas [chineseRemainder] ([2, 3, 2], [3, 5, 7])
      Out[1]=

      all solutions

      23 mod 105
      Domain
      ℕ
      Input (natural numbers)
      x ≡ 2 mod 3, x ≡ 3 mod 5, x ≡ 2 mod 7

      ✓ Checked by Lean’s kernel: all solutions

      23 < 105 ∧ ∀ (y : ℕ), (∀ p ∈ [(3, 2), (5, 3), (7, 2)], y ≡ p.2 [MOD p.1]) ↔ y ≡ 23 [MOD 105]
      The certificate: 1 checked step

      1. all solutions

        23 < 105 ∧ ∀ (y : ℕ), (∀ p ∈ [(3, 2), (5, 3), (7, 2)], y ≡ p.2 [MOD p.1]) ↔ y ≡ 23 [MOD 105]

        Checked by Lean’s kernel

      A modular power with an exponent of 27 digits. The proof is repeated squaring, so it never builds the full power, and its size grows with the number of digits of the exponent:

      In[2]:=
      #cas (powerMod 2 123456789123456789123456789 1000000007)
      Out[2]=

      modular power

      612059118
      Domain
      ℕ
      Input (natural numbers)
      2^123456789123456789123456789 mod 1000000007

      ✓ Checked by Lean’s kernel: modular power

      2 ^ 123456789123456789123456789 % 1000000007 = 612059118
      The certificate: 1 checked step

      1. modular power

        2 ^ 123456789123456789123456789 % 1000000007 = 612059118

        Checked by Lean’s kernel

      Without Hex, primeQ, factorInteger and moebiusMu refuse, and the message says how to turn Hex on. Even with Hex the factor search has a budget, and when it runs out the command claims nothing. The divisor commands certify n<1012, eulerPhi without Hex counts up to n≤100000, and binomial, factorial and fibonacci compute values of at most 10000 digits. continuedFraction takes rational numbers only, since an irrational number has an infinite expansion.
