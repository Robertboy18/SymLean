      Documentation › Sums and recurrences › product

      Mode of #cas

      product

      A finite product in closed form, telescoped or in factorials, with a theorem for every upper bound.

      #cas [product k a n] e
      gives the product of e for k from the natural number a to the variable n in closed form, and proves it for every n by induction.

      product looks for a closed form of a finite product with a symbolic upper bound, and proves it for every n by induction. The theorem is about Mathlib’s ∏ k ∈ Finset.Icc a n, and the factorials in the answer are Nat.factorial.

      The factors it takes are a constant times powers of linear factors k+s with integer shifts s. When the exponents add up to zero, the product telescopes to a rational function of n, and when every shift is nonnegative, it is a product of factorials.

      Details and Options

      • For the printed closed form P, the theorem is ∀ n : ℕ, ∏ k ∈ Finset.Icc a n, e = P, and ∀ n : ℕ, a - 1 ≤ n → ∏ k ∈ Finset.Icc a n, e = P when a≥2. The product over an empty range is 1.
      • e is a rational function of k. Without Hex its linear factors must be written in the input, so (k - 1) * (k + 1) / k ^ 2 has a closed form and 1 - 1 / k ^ 2 is refused. With import CASHex.Factor, the numerator and the denominator are factored completely over ℚ.
      • A factor that vanishes in the range is refused, and so are a rational shift such as 2*k - 1 and an irreducible factor of degree 2 or more: the factor must be a constant times powers of linear factors k+s with an integer s.
      • The upper bound must be a variable. There are no options.
      • In a proof, cas proves the closed form for every n.

      Applications (1)

      The closed form in a proof. cas proves it by induction, and only the three standard axioms are used:

      In[10]:=
      theorem product_telescoping : ∀ n : ℕ, ∏ k ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, ((k : ℝ) + 1) / k = n + 1 := by
  cas

#print axioms product_telescoping
      Out[10]=

      product_telescoping depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (6)

      Without Hex the factors must be written out, and 1−1/k2 is the product from 2 above in another form:

      In[12]:=
      #cas [product k 2 n] (1 - 1/k^2)
      Out[12]=

      Error

      product: the factor is not, as written, a constant times powers of linear factors k + s with integer s, and without Hex `product` does not factor it: write it as such a product, or use CASHex (`lake -R -Khex=on build CASHex`, `import CASHex.Factor`), which factors it completely over ℚ

      With Hex, CASHex.Factor factors it, and the closed form is the same. This line is from the library’s Hex examples, with the output it pins:

      In[13]:=
      #cas [product k 2 n] (1 - 1/k^2)

      Quoted from CASHex/Examples.lean, where #guard_msgs pins the output (the part shown).

      Out[13]=

      closed form

      (n + 1)/(2*n) for n ≥ 1

      A rational shift is refused, here 2k−1=2(k−1/2), since the shifts must be integers:

      In[14]:=
      #cas [product k 1 n] (2*k - 1)
      Out[14]=

      Error

      product: the factor is not a constant times powers of linear factors k + s with integer s; its product needs Gamma functions or Pochhammer symbols

      The factor k2+1 is irreducible over ℚ. Without Hex the message suggests factoring it, and with Hex it is refused for the same reason as a rational shift:

      In[15]:=
      #cas [product k 1 n] (k^2 + 1)
      Out[15]=

      Error

      product: the factor is not, as written, a constant times powers of linear factors k + s with integer s, and without Hex `product` does not factor it: write it as such a product, or use CASHex (`lake -R -Khex=on build CASHex`, `import CASHex.Factor`), which factors it completely over ℚ
      In[16]:=
      #cas [product k 1 n] (k^2 + 1)

      Quoted from CASHex/Examples.lean, where #guard_msgs pins the output.

      Out[16]=

      Error

      product: the factor is not a constant times powers of linear factors k + s with integer s; its product needs Gamma functions or Pochhammer symbols

      A factor that vanishes in the range is refused, here the denominator k at k=0:

      In[17]:=
      #cas [product k 0 n] (1 / k)
      Out[17]=

      Error

      product: the factor k vanishes in the range of the product

      The upper bound must be a variable. For the value 5!, the call factorial is the one to use:

      In[18]:=
      #cas [product k 1 5] (k)
      Out[18]=

      Error

      product: the upper bound must be a variable

      Only rational functions of k:

      In[19]:=
      #cas [product k 1 n] (sin k)
      Out[19]=

      Error

      product: the expression is not a rational function: it has a function other than the field operations and natural powers
