      Function of #cas

      zeta

      Exact values of the Riemann zeta function at even positive and nonpositive integers, with a theorem about Mathlib’s riemannZeta.

      #cas (zeta s)
      gives the exact value of ζ(s) at an even positive or a nonpositive integer s and proves it as an equation about riemannZeta s in ℂ.

      zeta gives exact values of the Riemann zeta function at the even positive integers and at the nonpositive integers. At an even positive integer the value is a rational multiple of πs, as in Euler’s ζ(2)=π2/6, and at a nonpositive integer it is rational. Both come from Bernoulli numbers that the kernel evaluates. The call has no formula for the odd integers from 3 on, and refuses them.

      Details and Options

      • s is a closed rational expression whose value is an integer, such as 2 or (-1), with −59≤s≤60.
      • At an even s=2k>0 the theorem is riemannZeta s = a * (Real.pi : ℂ) ^ s / b with a/b in lowest terms, and without a * when a=1, by Mathlib’s riemannZeta_two_mul_nat, such as riemannZeta 2 = (Real.pi : ℂ) ^ 2 / 6.
      • At an integer s≤0 the theorem is riemannZeta s = q with a rational q, by riemannZeta_neg_nat_eq_bernoulli.
      • The equations hold in ℂ, for Mathlib’s riemannZeta, and their rational number comes from a Bernoulli number that the kernel evaluates.
      • The odd integers s≥3 and the pole s=1 are refused, and so are rationals that are not integers.
      • The call is evaluated when it is the whole input of #cas.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and use it:

      In[6]:=
      theorem six_mul_zeta_two (h : riemannZeta 2 = (Real.pi : ℂ) ^ 2 / 6) :
    6 * riemannZeta 2 = (Real.pi : ℂ) ^ 2 := by
  rw [h]
  ring

#print axioms six_mul_zeta_two
      Out[6]=

      six_mul_zeta_two depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (3)

      At the odd integers s≥3 the call has no formula and refuses:

      In[10]:=
      #cas (zeta 3)
      Out[10]=

      Error

      zeta: unsupported formula at the odd integer `3`; exact values are computed at even positive and at nonpositive integers

      s=1 is the pole. Mathlib’s riemannZeta 1 is a convention, not a value of the series, and the call does not report it:

      In[11]:=
      #cas (zeta 1)
      Out[11]=

      Error

      zeta: s = 1 is the pole of ζ; Mathlib's riemannZeta 1 is a convention, not a value of the series, and is not reported

      The argument is an integer:

      In[12]:=
      #cas (zeta (1/2))
      Out[12]=

      Error

      zeta: exact values are computed at integers
