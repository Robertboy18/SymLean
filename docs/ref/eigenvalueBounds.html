      Documentation › Linear algebra › eigenvalueBounds

      Mode of #cas

      eigenvalueBounds

      Decimal bounds on the smallest and the largest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix, with a theorem that they hold and are sharp.

      #cas [eigenvalueBounds] A
      gives the smallest and the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric matrix A with rational entries, rounded outward to 6 decimal places, and proves the bounds and that they are sharp.
      #cas (digits := d) [eigenvalueBounds] A
      gives the same bounds to d decimal places.

      For a symmetric matrix, the extreme eigenvalues are the extreme values of xTAx on the unit sphere, and in a proof that is usually the form you need: c|x|2≤xTAx≤μ|x|2 for every x. eigenvalueBounds proves exactly this, with decimals c and μ. It also proves that neither bound improves by 10−d, so the bounds are as tight as the digits allow. I find this most useful when the exact eigenvalues are roots of a high-degree polynomial and the argument only needs a bound on the quadratic form.

      Details and Options

      • A is a symmetric square matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]]. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • With Q(x)=∑i∑jAijxixj for the real matrix A and the printed decimals c and μ, the theorem says three things over ℝ:
        • c * ∑ i, x i ^ 2 ≤ Q x ∧ Q x ≤ μ * ∑ i, x i ^ 2 for every x : Fin n → ℝ;
        • Q x < (c + 10 ^ (-d)) * ∑ i, x i ^ 2 for some x;
        • (μ - 10 ^ (-d)) * ∑ i, x i ^ 2 < Q x for some x.
      • The theorem writes Q(x) out as ∑ i, ∑ j, A i j * x i * x j, and the numbers as decimals. So c is the smallest eigenvalue rounded down to d places, and μ the largest one rounded up.
      • Both bounds come from bisection on the grid 10−dℤ. The kernel checks exact factorizations LDLT over ℚ with nonnegative pivots of A−cI and μI−A, and the two rational vectors that show the bounds are sharp.
      • (digits := d) sets the number d of decimal places: 6 by default, between 1 and cas.maxDigits, which is 2000 by default (see Options).

      Applications (1)

      A proof can take the first part of the printed theorem as a hypothesis and use its lower bound:

      In[8]:=
      theorem rayleigh_lower
    (h : ∀ x : Fin 2 → ℝ,
      1.000000 * ∑ i, x i ^ 2 ≤ ∑ i, ∑ j, !![(2 : ℝ), 1; 1, 2] i j * x i * x j ∧
        ∑ i, ∑ j, !![(2 : ℝ), 1; 1, 2] i j * x i * x j ≤ 3.000000 * ∑ i, x i ^ 2)
    (x : Fin 2 → ℝ) :
    ∑ i, x i ^ 2 ≤ ∑ i, ∑ j, !![(2 : ℝ), 1; 1, 2] i j * x i * x j := by
  have e : (1.000000 : ℝ) = 1 := by norm_num
  have h₁ := (h x).1
  rw [e, one_mul] at h₁
  exact h₁

#print axioms rayleigh_lower
      Out[8]=

      rayleigh_lower depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
