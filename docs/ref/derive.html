      Documentation › Calculus › derive

      Mode of #cas

      derive

      The derivative of an expression, with the conditions it needs and a theorem about Mathlib’s HasDerivAt.

      #cas [derive x] e
      gives the derivative ∂e/∂x of e with respect to x, with the conditions under which it holds. Every other variable of e is a parameter.
      #cas [derive] e
      differentiates e in its only variable, or a function fun x ↦ e in its parameter.

      derive computes a derivative and gives its HasDerivAt statement. You get the derivative printed the way you would write it, and under it the theorem SymLean proved: a statement about Mathlib’s HasDerivAt for the real functions, with every condition it needs as a hypothesis. For me those conditions are as much a part of the answer as the derivative. The derivative of 2x+2 is claimed only where 2x+2>0, and the derivative of 1/x only where x≠0.

      Details and Options

      • The theorem has the form ∀x,c1→…→ck→HasDerivAt(λx.e)e′x, where e′ is the printed derivative and c1,…,ck are the printed conditions. Lean’s kernel checks its proof before the report appears.
      • The functions are abs, sin, cos, tan, sinh, cosh, tanh, atan, asin, acos, exp, log, sqrt and erf, with +, -, *, / and powers. A power with a variable exponent, such as x ^ y, is a real power.
      • A square root contributes a condition a>0 and a quotient a condition d≠0. Lean’s division is total (1/0=0), so a derivative of 1/x is only claimed where x≠0.
      • A function fun t ↦ e is differentiated in its parameter. Name it, as in #cas [derive t], or leave the name out.
      • set_option cas.verbose true adds the tree of differentiation rules to the report: each rule with its input and its raw derivative.
      • set_option cas.suggest true adds the theorem as an example you can paste into your file in place of the command.
      • The tactic cas proves the same HasDerivAt and deriv goals, so a derivative you found here can go straight into a proof.
      • Mathlib’s deriv is total: at a point where the function is not differentiable it is 0. For the bare functions abs, Real.log, Real.sinh, Real.cosh and Real.tan, cas applies Mathlib’s deriv lemmas, which hold at every point, so it proves equalities such as deriv Real.log 0 = 0⁻¹ (both sides are 0) without a validity check. A HasDerivAt goal, by contrast, states differentiability.
