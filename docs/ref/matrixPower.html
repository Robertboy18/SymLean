      Documentation › Linear algebra › matrixPower

      Mode of #cas

      matrixPower

      The power Ak of a square matrix with rational entries, for k up to 1000, with a theorem that it is right.

      #cas [matrixPower k] A
      gives the power Ak of the square matrix A with rational entries, for a natural number k up to 1000, and proves A ^ k = B.

      A linear recurrence or a Markov chain takes the same linear step over and over, so k steps of it are the k-th power of a matrix, and matrixPower computes that power for the k you choose. A good first example is (1110), whose powers hold the Fibonacci numbers. The theorem A ^ k = B is about Mathlib’s power in the ring of matrices over ℚ, and the kernel decides it by evaluating the product.

      Details and Options

      • k is a natural number literal with k≤1000. A is a square matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]], with closed rational entries such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • The theorem is A ^ k = B for the matrices over ℚ, decided by kernel evaluation (decide +kernel).
      • In a proof, decide +kernel proves the printed equation. A larger exponent splits with pow_add and pow_mul into powers that the mode computes.
      • The exponent is a natural number. For a negative power, take a power of the matrix that inverse prints.

      Applications (3)

      The power as a theorem. Kernel evaluation, the proof behind the report, proves it directly:

      In[6]:=
      theorem fibonacci_matrix : !![(1 : ℚ), 1; 1, 0] ^ 10 = !![89, 55; 55, 34] := by
  decide +kernel

#print axioms fibonacci_matrix
      Out[6]=

      fibonacci_matrix depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      The kernel evaluates the power, and the exponent is at most 1000:

      In[14]:=
      #cas [matrixPower 1001] !![1, 1; 1, 0]
      Out[14]=

      Error

      matrixPower 1001: the power is decided by kernel evaluation, up to exponent 1000

      The matrix has to be square:

      In[15]:=
      #cas [matrixPower 2] !![1, 2, 3; 4, 5, 6]
      Out[15]=

      Error

      matrixPower 2: the matrix must be square
