      Documentation › Number theory › binomial

      Function of #cas

      binomial

      The binomial coefficient (nk) of natural numbers n and k, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.choose.

      #cas (binomial n k)
      gives the binomial coefficient (nk) of the natural numbers n and k and proves Nat.choose n k = c.

      binomial computes a binomial coefficient (nk). It is a small thing, but the value comes with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.choose, so a proof can use the number the report prints instead of a number copied over by hand.

      Details and Options

      • n and k are natural numbers, literals or closed integer expressions such as 2 ^ 10. Negative and non-integer arguments are refused, since Nat.choose is defined on natural numbers.
      • The theorem is Nat.choose n k = c. For k≤n the proof rewrites with Nat.choose_eq_descFactorial_div_factorial, after Nat.choose_symm when n−k<k, and norm_num evaluates n(n−1)⋯(n−j+1)/j! for the smaller j of k and n−k.
      • For k>n the proof applies Nat.choose_eq_zero_of_lt, and the value is 0.
      • The value has at most 10000 digits, the most that a call computes.
      • The call is evaluated when it is the whole input of #cas.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      In a proof, cas proves the equation, here without any axioms:

      In[4]:=
      theorem choose_ten_three : Nat.choose 10 3 = 120 := by
  cas

#print axioms choose_ten_three
      Out[4]=

      choose_ten_three depends on no axioms.

      Possible Issues (4)

      Mathlib’s Ring.choose extends (nk) to negative n, and the call evaluates Nat.choose, so a negative argument is refused:

      In[6]:=
      #cas (binomial (-3) 2)
      Out[6]=

      Error

      binomial takes a natural number as its first argument; `-3` is negative

      The arguments are numbers, not variables:

      In[7]:=
      #cas (binomial x 2)
      Out[7]=

      Error

      binomial takes integer arguments; `x` is a variable

      The call takes two arguments:

      In[8]:=
      #cas (binomial 10)
      Out[8]=

      Error

      binomial takes 2 arguments, as in `binomial n k`; the call has 1

      The value has at most 10000 digits, and (10000050000) has 30101:

      In[9]:=
      #cas (binomial 100000 50000)
      Out[9]=

      Error

      binomial: the value has more than 10000 digits, the most that a call computes
