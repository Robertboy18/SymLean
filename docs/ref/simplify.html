      Documentation › Algebra › simplify

      Mode of #cas

      simplify

      Simplifies a real expression by checked rewrites, with the conditions under which the result equals it.

      #cas [simplify] e
      simplifies the real expression e by exact rewrites and gives the conditions under which the result equals e.

      simplify reduces an expression and tells you when the result equals it. Every rewrite is checked, and the theorem under the answer proves the equality wherever the printed conditions hold. The conditions are sufficient, not necessary, so the result can also equal the input where they fail. x2 simplifies to x under x>0, for example, and the two agree at x=0 as well.

      The conditions are there because Lean’s division is total: 1/0=0. At x=1 the quotient (x4−1)/(x2−1) is 0/0=0 in Lean, while x2+1 is 2. So the identity holds where x2−1≠0, and that is the condition the theorem carries. Without that condition the identity is false in Lean, and that is easy to miss when you copy an answer from a computer algebra system into a proof.

      Details and Options

      • The theorem has the form ∀x1,…,xn,c1→…→ck→e=e′ about Mathlib’s real functions, where e′ is the printed result and c1,…,ck are the printed conditions. For x + x it is ∀ (x : ℝ), x + x = 2 * x.
      • “The certificate” shows two steps: that theorem, and the same statement for SymLean’s internal expressions, from which it is transported. When the proof of the first takes more than its own budget of maxHeartbeats heartbeats, the report keeps only the internal statement.
      • The variables are real. The functions are abs, sin, cos, tan, sinh, cosh, tanh, atan, asin, acos, exp, log and sqrt, with +, -, *, / and powers. A power with a variable exponent is a real power.
      • Cancelling a divisor d adds the condition d≠0, and combining real powers of a adds the condition a>0.
      • A condition is printed as a polynomial only when it equals that polynomial for every value of its variables. Otherwise it keeps the form it has in the input.
      • set_option cas.suggest true adds the theorem as an example to paste in place of the command, when that example elaborates in your file to the same statement.
      • A bare #cas e with no mode also simplifies (see #cas). fullSimplify tries many more forms and keeps the simplest.
      • The tactic cas proves the same identities, with the conditions as hypotheses.
