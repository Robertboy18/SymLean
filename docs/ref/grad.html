      Documentation › Calculus › grad

      Mode of #cas

      grad

      The gradient of an expression in several variables, with a theorem about Mathlib’s HasGradientAt.

      #cas [grad x, y, …] e
      gives the gradient of e in the listed variables, in that order, with a theorem about Mathlib’s HasGradientAt and the conditions it needs.

      grad collects the partial derivatives of a function into one vector. The theorem is about Mathlib’s HasGradientAt at the point (x1,…,xn) of EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n), since that is where gradients live in Mathlib. Every component is also proved on its own, as with derive, and I find that handy: when a proof needs one partial derivative, you don’t have to dig it out of the vector.

      Details and Options

      • For the variables x1,…,xn, the printed components g1,…,gn and the conditions c1,…,ck, the theorem is ∀x1…xn,c1→…→ck→HasGradientAt(λp.e)(g1,…,gn)(x1,…,xn). Lean writes the vectors as !₂[g₁, …, gₙ] and e in the coordinates p.ofLp 0, p.ofLp 1, … of the point p.
      • The vector of the components is also Mathlib’s gradient at every point where e is defined: every divisor and every argument of log is nonzero, every argument of sqrt and every base of a real power is positive, and no argument of asin or acos is 1 or −1.
      • Every variable of e must be listed, and the order in brackets is the order of the components. For a partial derivative with the other variables as parameters, derive is the mode.
      • The functions are those of derive.
      • set_option cas.verbose true shows the tree of differentiation rules of every component, and set_option cas.suggest true adds the theorem as an example to paste.
