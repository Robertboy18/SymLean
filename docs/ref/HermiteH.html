      Function of #cas

      HermiteH

      The physicists’ Hermite polynomial Hn in monomials, proved at every real x, and its exact values at rational points.

      #cas (HermiteH n x)
      gives the physicists’ Hermite polynomial Hn in the variable x, in monomials with integer coefficients, and proves the expansion at every real x.
      #cas (HermiteH n q)
      gives the exact value of Hn at the rational number q and proves it.
      #cas [N d] (HermiteH n q)
      gives the exact value of Hn at q with its first d significant digits, truncated toward zero, and proves their decimal bounds.

      There are two families of Hermite polynomials in common use, and HermiteH gives the physicists’ ones, Hn, with the convention of Mathematica’s HermiteH. Mathlib defines only the probabilists’ polynomials Hen, as Polynomial.hermite n, so the theorem cannot name Hn directly. It states Hn through them, as 2nHen(2x), and you will see those factors of 2 in every statement. HermiteHe gives Hen itself.

      Details and Options

      • n is a natural number with n≤60, a literal or a closed integer expression.
      • The second argument is a variable, which the polynomial is written in and which may have any name other than pi, π, Pi, e and E, or a rational number.
      • Hn(x)=2nHen(2x). For the printed polynomial p the theorem is ∀ x : ℝ, √2 ^ n * Polynomial.aeval (√2 * x) (Polynomial.hermite n) = p, by CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.sqrt_two_pow_mul_aeval_hermite.
      • At a rational q the theorem is √2 ^ n * Polynomial.aeval (√2 * q) (Polynomial.hermite n) = v. With [N d] it adds the first d significant digits of v, truncated toward zero, and their decimal bounds. The report prints v = digits when the digits are exact and v ≈ digits otherwise.
      • With [N d], 1≤d≤2000, as for N.
      • #cas [expand] (HermiteH n x) prints the same polynomial as #cas (HermiteH n x).
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and specialize it, here at x=0:

      In[6]:=
      theorem hermiteH_four_at_zero
    (h : ∀ x : ℝ, √2 ^ 4 * Polynomial.aeval (√2 * x) (Polynomial.hermite 4) =
      16 * x ^ 4 - 48 * x ^ 2 + 12) :
    √2 ^ 4 * Polynomial.aeval (√2 * 0) (Polynomial.hermite 4) = 12 := by
  rw [h 0]
  norm_num

#print axioms hermiteH_four_at_zero
      Out[6]=

      hermiteH_four_at_zero depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      The degree is at most 60. #cas (HermiteHe n x) accepts degrees up to 80, and Hn(x)=2nHen(2x) relates the two:

      In[8]:=
      #cas (HermiteH 61 x)
      Out[8]=

      Error

      HermiteH accepts degrees up to 60

      The call takes two arguments, the degree and then the variable or the point:

      In[9]:=
      #cas (HermiteH 3)
      Out[9]=

      Error

      HermiteH takes 2 arguments, as in `HermiteH n x`; the call has 1
