      Documentation › Number theory › powerMod

      Function of #cas

      powerMod

      The remainder of ab modulo m by repeated squaring, for exponents with hundreds of digits, with a theorem in ℕ.

      #cas (powerMod a b m)
      gives the remainder of a^b modulo m for natural numbers a, b and a positive modulus m, and proves a ^ b % m = r.

      powerMod computes ab modulo m without ever building the integer ab, which for a large exponent would be far too big to write down. The proof is repeated squaring, so its size grows with the number of digits of the exponent and not with the exponent itself. Exponents with hundreds of digits are fine.

      Details and Options

      • a, b and m are natural numbers, literals or closed integer expressions, with m≥1.
      • The theorem is a ^ b % m = r in ℕ, with r<m. The proof is Mathlib’s Mathlib.Meta.NormNum.evalNatPowMod, repeated squaring.
      • The call is evaluated when it is the whole input of #cas.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      In a proof, cas proves the equation, with propext as its only axiom:

      In[3]:=
      theorem three_pow_mod_seven : 3 ^ 1000 % 7 = 4 := by
  cas

#print axioms three_pow_mod_seven
      Out[3]=

      three_pow_mod_seven depends on the axioms propext.

      Properties and Relations (2)

      Fermat’s little theorem gives ap−1≡1(modp) for a prime p that does not divide a. Here 26≡1(mod7) and 100=6·16+4, so 2100≡24=16≡2:

      Out[4]=

      Possible Issues (2)

      Every natural number is 0 modulo 1, and Mathlib’s a % 0 = a. The call asks for m≥1:

      In[6]:=
      #cas (powerMod 2 10 0)
      Out[6]=

      Error

      powerMod: the modulus must be positive

      The call takes three arguments:

      In[7]:=
      #cas (powerMod 2 10)
      Out[7]=

      Error

      powerMod takes 3 arguments, as in `powerMod a b m`; the call has 2
