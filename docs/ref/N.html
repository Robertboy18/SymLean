      Mode of #cas

      N

      The first digits of a closed real expression, with a theorem that every printed digit is right.

      #cas [N d] e
      gives the first d significant digits of the closed real expression e, truncated toward zero, with a theorem that they are the digits of the real number.

      When I ask for a number, I want to know that its digits are right. N gives decimal approximations with an explicit error bound, and the digits are not a floating point guess. The report proves that the value of e lies between the printed decimal and the next one away from zero, as a statement about Mathlib’s Real.pi, Real.exp and Real.sqrt, and Lean’s kernel checks that proof. So if the report says that 2 begins 1.41421, it does.

      Details and Options

      • For a positive value whose printed digits form the integer m, the theorem is m/10k≤e<(m+1)/10k. For a negative value it is −(m+1)/10k<e≤−m/10k. A large value has m·10k in place of m/10k.
      • Truncation cannot decide the digits of a value with a short decimal expansion, such as cos(π/3)=12. When four doublings of the working precision do not settle them, the report proves a decimal enclosure lo/10k≤e≤hi/10k instead.
      • e has no variables. It may use rational arithmetic, integer powers, sqrt, exp, log, sin, cos, tan, atan, asin, acos, the hyperbolic functions, real powers with a positive base, and the constants pi (or π) and E.
      • BesselJ n q, erf q and the orthogonal polynomials at a rational point q have routes of their own. BesselJ and erf give at most 30 digits.
      • d lies between 1 and the option cas.maxDigits, which is 2000 unless you set it.
      • The tactic cega proves the bounds that the digits imply, such as Real.sqrt 2 ≤ 1.415.

      Possible Issues (3)

      N evaluates closed expressions only. For an expression with a variable, enclose works on a box:

      In[18]:=
      #cas [N 15] (x + 1)
      Out[18]=

      Error

      N needs a closed expression; free symbols: x

      At least one digit, and at most cas.maxDigits:

      In[19]:=
      #cas [N 0] (pi)
      Out[19]=

      Error

      N accepts between 1 and 2000 digits
      In[20]:=
      set_option cas.maxDigits 10 in
#cas [N 20] (pi)
      Out[20]=

      Error

      N accepts between 1 and 10 digits

      The series of erf and BesselJ stop at 30 digits:

      In[21]:=
      #cas [N 31] (erf 1)
      Out[21]=

      Error

      erf accepts at most 30 digits
