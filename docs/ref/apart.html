      Documentation › Algebra › apart

      Mode of #cas

      apart

      Writes a rational function of one variable as a polynomial plus partial fractions, with the conditions of the identity.

      #cas [apart] e
      writes the rational function e of one variable as a polynomial plus a sum of partial fractions and gives the conditions under which they agree.
      #cas [apart x] e
      does the same with x named as the variable.

      apart writes a rational function as a polynomial plus partial fractions. It undoes together, and it plays the part of Mathematica’s Apart. Partial fractions are worth having for two reasons: they show the poles of a function at a glance, and they often make it easier to integrate.

      The thing to watch is the poles themselves. The theorem holds where the divisors of the input are nonzero, and it has to say so, because Lean’s division is total. At a pole both sides are computed with 1/0=0, and they differ. At x=1, for instance, the fraction 1/((x−1)(x+1)) is 0 in Lean, while 12(x−1)−12(x+1) is −14.

      Details and Options

      • The theorem has the form ∀x,d1≠0→…→dk≠0→e=q+∑c/pk, where d1,…,dk are the divisors of e as written, q is a polynomial, each p is a factor of the denominator with integer coefficients, k runs up to its multiplicity and degc<degp. Each p divides one of the di, which ring shows, and field_simp and ring prove the identity.
      • The theorem does not state that the factors are irreducible.
      • The input is a rational function of one variable with rational coefficients. #cas [apart x] e names the variable.
      • Without Hex the factors are those written in the input, refined by gcds: 1/((x - 1)*(x + 1)) splits, and a factor written as x^2 - 1 stays whole. When a factor of degree two or more remains, the label reads “partial fractions over the written factors, refined by gcds”.
      • When the denominator is one nonlinear factor with exponent 1, only the polynomial part is split off, and a proper fraction with such a denominator is refused.
      • With CASHex.Factor imported (built by lake -R -Khex=on build CASHex), Hex factors the denominator completely over ℚ. The Hex guide has the details.
      • The tactic cas proves these identities, with the conditions as hypotheses.

      Possible Issues (3)

      Without Hex, a proper fraction whose denominator is written as one nonlinear factor is refused. Write the denominator as (x - 1)*(x + 1), as in the first example, or import CASHex.Factor:

      In[16]:=
      #cas [apart] (1/(x^2-1))
      Out[16]=

      Error

      apart: the denominator x^2 - 1 is a single factor as written and the fraction is proper, and without Hex `apart` does not factor it: write it as a product of its factors, or use CASHex (`lake -R -Khex=on build CASHex`, `import CASHex.Factor`), which factors it completely over ℚ

      The mode works in one variable:

      In[17]:=
      #cas [apart] (1/(x*y))
      Out[17]=

      Error

      apart: expected a rational function of one variable, found the variables x, y
