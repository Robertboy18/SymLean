      Documentation › Linear algebra › eigenvectors

      Mode of #cas

      eigenvectors

      A basis of each eigenspace of a square matrix with rational entries, with a theorem for every eigenvalue, irrational ones included.

      #cas [eigenvectors] A
      gives a basis of the eigenspace of each eigenvalue of the square matrix A with rational entries, and proves it for every eigenvalue, the irrational ones included.

      For a rational eigenvalue λ there isn’t much to say: eigenvectors computes the null space of A−λI and proves it with the theorem of nullSpace. The irrational eigenvalues are where it gets interesting. Take a factor q of the characteristic polynomial. You get one basis that serves all the roots α of q at once, with entries that are polynomials in α, and one theorem that holds for every root of q in ℝ or ℂ. That’s how I would write these eigenvectors by hand, and it means nobody has to read nested radicals.

      Details and Options

      • A is a square matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]]. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • For a rational eigenvalue λ with printed vectors Vi, the theorem is the statement of nullSpace for A - λ • 1 over ℚ: (A - λ • 1).mulVec (V i) = 0 for each i, LinearIndependent ℚ V, and ∀ x, (A - λ • 1).mulVec x = 0 → ∃ c, x = ∑ i, c i • V i.
      • The other eigenvalues are the roots of the monic factors q of A.charpoly left after the rational roots. For each q, the field K is ℝ when a certificate shows that every root of q is real, and ℂ otherwise, and M is A over K. The theorem is ∀ α : K, Polynomial.aeval α q = 0 → (∀ i, (M - α • 1).mulVec (V i) = 0) ∧ LinearIndependent K V ∧ ∀ x, (M - α • 1).mulVec x = 0 → ∃ c, x = ∑ i, c i • V i.
      • For K=ℝ the theorem also says (q.map (algebraMap ℚ ℝ)).Splits, so that the real roots are all the roots of q. The certificate is the discriminant of a quadratic, or intervals around the roots that do not overlap, and the kernel checks it (CAS.Analysis.Matrix.EigFactor.splits_of_realCheck).
      • The theorem ends with the factorization of A.charpoly in ℚ[X] into the factors X−λ and q, with their multiplicities.
      • Without Hex, a factor q of degree 3 or more comes from the squarefree decomposition and need not be irreducible. The elimination over ℚ[X]/(q) may then split q, and each part gets its own statement. With the optional Hex library (import CASHex.Factor), the theorem adds Irreducible q for each q whose irreducibility Hex certifies, and the report lists the others.
      • There is no limit on the size of A. The search runs before the proof, and the proof runs with maxHeartbeats raised to at least one million, so a large matrix can take long.
