      Documentation › Differential equations › fourierSin

      Mode of #cas

      fourierSin

      The coefficients of the sine series on [0, L] as exact functions of n, with a theorem about the integrals.

      #cas [fourierSin x n L] f
      gives the coefficients bn of the sine series of f on the interval from 0 to L, as exact functions of n for n≥1, and proves the integrals.

      The sine series on [0,L] is the one that comes up for a vibrating string with fixed ends, and fourierSin computes its coefficients. The series is ∑n≥1bnsin(nπx/L), and the theorem is about Mathlib’s interval integrals over [0,L], one for every n≥1.

      Each term cxjeax of f is integrated by parts, and cosnπ=(−1)n gives the factors (−1)n you see in the result. For the cosine series on the same interval, use fourierCos, and for the full series on [−π,π], use fourier.

      Details and Options

      • For the printed bₙ, the theorem is that for every n : ℕ with 0 < n, 2 / L * ∫ x in 0..L, f * Real.sin (n * Real.pi * x / L) = bₙ. It is printed in simplified form, such as Real.sin (n * x) for L=π.
      • f is a sum of terms c * x ^ j * exp (a * x) with rational a, and π may appear in the coefficients c, as in x * (pi - x). Sines, cosines and other functions are refused.
      • L is a positive rational number or a positive rational multiple of π, such as 2 * pi / 3.
      • When the proof of the coefficients fails, the command is refused.
      • There are no options. In a proof, cas proves the integral behind a single coefficient, as under Applications.

      Applications (1)

      The integral behind b1=8/π of x(π−x), in a proof. cas proves it, and only the three standard axioms are used:

      In[4]:=
      theorem fourierSin_b_one : ∫ x in (0 : ℝ)..Real.pi, x * (Real.pi - x) * Real.sin x = 4 := by
  cas

#print axioms fourierSin_b_one
      Out[4]=

      fourierSin_b_one depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Properties and Relations (2)

      Possible Issues (2)

      The length must be positive:

      In[7]:=
      #cas [fourierSin x n 0] (x)
      Out[7]=

      Error

      fourierSin: L must be positive

      Other functions are refused on [0,L], here ex2. fourier takes sines and cosines with integer frequencies on [−π,π]:

      In[8]:=
      #cas [fourierSin x n pi] (exp (x ^ 2))
      Out[8]=

      Error

      fourierSin: on [0, L] the function must be a sum of terms c x ^ j e ^ (a x)
