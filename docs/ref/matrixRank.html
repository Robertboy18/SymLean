      Mode of #cas

      matrixRank

      The rank of a matrix of any shape with rational entries, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Matrix.rank.

      #cas [matrixRank] A
      gives the rank of the matrix A with rational entries, and proves A.rank = r.

      The rank of a matrix is the number of its independent rows, which is also the number of its independent columns. You can use matrixRank to check that some vectors are independent, or to count how many conditions in a linear system are really independent. The theorem is about Mathlib’s Matrix.rank over ℚ, and the interesting part is how it is proved. A rank is an equality, so it needs a bound from each side, and the kernel checks one certificate for each. A factorization A=CF through r columns bounds the rank from above, and an r×r submatrix with nonzero determinant bounds it from below.

      Details and Options

      • A is a matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]] of any shape. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • The theorem is A.rank = r for Mathlib’s Matrix.rank over ℚ. The columns of C are the pivot columns of A, and the kernel checks A=CF by evaluation (Matrix.rank_mul_le_right). The rows of the minor come from a second elimination, and its nonzero determinant gives the lower bound (Matrix.rank_of_det_ne_zero).
      • The tactic cas proves goals Matrix.rank A = r for matrix literals over ℚ, and refuses a wrong r.

      Applications (2)

      The rank as a theorem, with the axioms its proof uses:

      In[6]:=
      theorem rank_three : Matrix.rank !![(1 : ℚ), 2, 3; 4, 5, 6; 7, 8, 9] = 2 := by
  cas

#print axioms rank_three
      Out[6]=

      rank_three depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      A vector is not a matrix. Write it as a row !![1, 2, 3] or a column !![1; 2; 3]:

      In[13]:=
      #cas [matrixRank] ![1, 2, 3]
      Out[13]=

      Error

      expected a matrix `!![a, b; c, d]` or `[[a, b], [c, d]]`

      cas refuses a wrong rank:

      In[14]:=
      example : Matrix.rank !![(1 : ℚ), 2; 2, 4] = 2 := by
  cas
      Out[14]=

      Error

      cas: the rank is 1, not 2
