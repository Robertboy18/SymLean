      Documentation › Number theory › factorial

      Function of #cas

      factorial

      The factorial n! of a natural number, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Nat.factorial.

      #cas (factorial n)
      gives the factorial n!=1·2⋯n of the natural number n and proves Nat.factorial n = f.

      factorial computes n! and proves the value of Mathlib’s Nat.factorial. It’s a small mode, but the number you see is the number Mathlib means, so a proof can use it as it stands. When you need the factorial beyond the natural numbers, gamma extends it, with Γ(n+1)=n!.

      Details and Options

      • n is a natural number, a literal or a closed integer expression, and 0!=1.
      • The theorem is Nat.factorial n = f. norm_num proves it with Mathlib’s extension for factorials (Mathlib.Tactic.NormNum.NatFactorial), which splits the product in halves, and decide +kernel is the fallback.
      • An argument is evaluated with Lean’s arithmetic of its ascription, ℕ or ℤ. Other ascriptions are refused.
      • The value has at most 10000 digits, the most that a call computes.
      • The call is evaluated when it is the whole input of #cas.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      In a proof, cas proves the equation, here without any axioms:

      In[6]:=
      theorem factorial_ten : Nat.factorial 10 = 3628800 := by
  cas

#print axioms factorial_ten
      Out[6]=

      factorial_ten depends on no axioms.

      Possible Issues (3)

      Nat.factorial is defined on natural numbers, and a negative argument is refused:

      In[9]:=
      #cas (factorial (-1))
      Out[9]=

      Error

      factorial takes a natural number as its argument; `-1` is negative

      The ascriptions of an integer argument are ℕ and ℤ, and the ascription ℝ is refused even when the value is an integer:

      In[10]:=
      #cas (factorial (3 : ℝ))
      Out[10]=

      Error

      factorial takes integer arguments; `(3 : ℝ)` has the type `ℝ`, and the ascriptions of an integer argument are `ℕ` and `ℤ`

      The value has at most 10000 digits, and 5000! has 16326:

      In[11]:=
      #cas (factorial 5000)
      Out[11]=

      Error

      factorial: the value has more than 10000 digits, the most that a call computes
