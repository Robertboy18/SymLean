      Documentation › Calculus › integrate

      Mode of #cas

      integrate

      An antiderivative or a definite integral, checked by differentiation, with a theorem about Mathlib’s interval integral.

      #cas [integrate x] e
      finds an antiderivative F of e in x, checks it by differentiation, and proves ∫abedx=F(b)−F(a) on every interval where the printed conditions hold. Every other variable is a parameter.
      #cas [integrate] e
      integrates e in its only variable, or a function fun x ↦ e in its parameter.
      #cas [integrate x in [a, b]] e
      gives the integral of e from a to b, where an endpoint may be ∞ or -∞, with a theorem that states its value.

      integrate searches for an antiderivative and proves the integral formula that goes with it. Finding an antiderivative is a search, and searches go wrong, so SymLean differentiates every candidate and prints it only after its derivative matches the integrand. Differentiation is the easy direction, and that asymmetry is what makes the answer checkable.

      The theorem is the fundamental theorem of calculus for Mathlib’s interval integral ∫ x in a..b, with the printed conditions as a hypothesis on the whole interval. I would read those conditions before the formula. Conditions such as x>0 or logx≠0 are part of the answer, because they say on which intervals the formula holds.

      Details and Options

      • For the printed antiderivative F and conditions c1,…,ck the theorem is ∀ab,(∀x∈[a,b],c1∧…∧ck)→∫abedx=F(b)−F(a), stated with Set.uIcc a b, so the endpoints may come in either order. It holds for every value of the parameters, and the endpoints get other names when a variable is called a or b.
      • e uses the functions of derive. The search covers polynomials and a table of elementary antiderivatives, substitution, rational functions, integration by parts, polynomials times exponentials, sines and cosines, and rational functions of sinx and cosx. Gaussians ce−αx2+βx+γ are tried first and give the error function erf.
      • The search is bounded. When no candidate passes the derivative check, the command stops with an error and proves nothing.
      • Without the optional Hex library, a rational function is refused when its denominator keeps a factor of degree three or more after the powers of x are split off. With import CASHex.Factor the denominator is factored over ℚ.
      • The definite form gives F(b)−F(a) for the antiderivative that the search finds, without the Gaussian step. At an endpoint ∞ or -∞ it uses the limit of F, and the theorem says that the integrand is integrable over Set.Ioi a, Set.Iic b or the whole line and that its integral there is the value.
      • set_option cas.suggest true adds the theorem as an example you can paste in place of the command. set_option cas.verbose true adds the tree of differentiation rules that checked the antiderivative.
      • The tactic cas proves HasDerivAt goals, definite integrals ∫ x in a..b, e = v, improper integrals, and improper integrals with their integrability, IntegrableOn (fun x ↦ e) D ∧ ∫ x in D, e = v. When you already have a primitive, cas [integrate primitive := F] checks it.
