      Function of #cas

      LegendreP

      The Legendre polynomial Pn in monomials, proved at every real x, and its exact values at rational points.

      #cas (LegendreP n x)
      gives the Legendre polynomial Pn in the variable x, in monomials with exact rational coefficients, and proves the expansion at every real x.
      #cas (LegendreP n q)
      gives the exact value of Pn at the rational number q and proves it.
      #cas [N d] (LegendreP n q)
      gives the exact value of Pn at q with its first d significant digits, truncated toward zero, and proves their decimal bounds.

      LegendreP writes a Legendre polynomial in powers of its argument, or gives its exact value at a rational point. Its coefficients are rational, and they come out as exact fractions. The theorem holds at every real x for SymLean’s CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre n, which we define as Mathlib’s shifted Legendre polynomial at (1−x)/2, so it is the classical Pn, with Pn(1)=1.

      Details and Options

      • n is a natural number with n≤30, a literal or a closed integer expression.
      • The second argument is a variable, which the polynomial is written in and which may have any name other than pi, π, Pi, e and E, or a rational number.
      • For the printed polynomial p the theorem is ∀ x : ℝ, (CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre n).eval x = p, from the coefficient formula CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre_eval_eq_sum_coeff. The kernel checks each coefficient, a closed rational computation.
      • legendre n is Mathlib’s shifted Legendre polynomial Polynomial.shiftedLegendre n at (1−x)/2, so Pn(1)=1.
      • At a rational q the theorem is (CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre n).eval q = v, by CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre_eval_ratCast. With [N d] it adds the first d significant digits of v, truncated toward zero, and their decimal bounds. The report prints v = digits when the digits are exact and v ≈ digits otherwise.
      • With [N d], 1≤d≤2000, as for N.
      • #cas [expand] (LegendreP n x) prints the same polynomial as #cas (LegendreP n x).
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and specialize it, here at x=12:

      In[6]:=
      theorem legendre_three_at_half
    (h : ∀ x : ℝ,
      (CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre 3).eval x = 5 / 2 * x ^ 3 - 3 / 2 * x) :
    (CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.legendre 3).eval (1 / 2) = -7 / 16 := by
  rw [h]
  norm_num

#print axioms legendre_three_at_half
      Out[6]=

      legendre_three_at_half depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (3)

      The degree is at most 30. The bound keeps the rational coefficients short, and legendre n is defined at every degree:

      In[8]:=
      #cas (LegendreP 31 x)
      Out[8]=

      Error

      LegendreP accepts degrees up to 30

      A call computes with numbers of at most 10000 digits, and P2(105000)=3·1010000−12 is larger:

      In[9]:=
      #cas (LegendreP 2 (10 ^ 5000))
      Out[9]=

      Error

      LegendreP: evaluating `LegendreP 2 (10 ^ 5000)` needs a number with more than 10000 digits, the most that a call computes

      LegendreP is a call, not a mode, and the message lists its three forms:

      In[10]:=
      #cas [LegendreP 5]
      Out[10]=

      Error

      `LegendreP` is no mode of `#cas`; write `#cas (LegendreP n x)`, `#cas [expand] (LegendreP n x)` or `#cas [N d] (LegendreP n x)`
