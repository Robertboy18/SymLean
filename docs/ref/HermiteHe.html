      Function of #cas

      HermiteHe

      The probabilists’ Hermite polynomial Hen, Mathlib’s Polynomial.hermite n, in monomials, with its exact values at rational points.

      #cas (HermiteHe n x)
      gives the probabilists’ Hermite polynomial Hen in the variable x, in monomials with integer coefficients, and proves the expansion in ℤ[X].
      #cas (HermiteHe n q)
      gives the exact value of Hen at the rational number q and proves it.
      #cas [N d] (HermiteHe n q)
      gives the exact value of Hen at q with its first d significant digits, truncated toward zero, and proves their decimal bounds.

      HermiteHe gives the probabilists’ Hermite polynomials Hen, the ones SymPy calls hermite_prob. They are Mathlib’s own Polynomial.hermite n, so the theorem is as plain as it can be: an equation of polynomials in ℤ[X], with nothing rescaled on the way. HermiteH gives the physicists’ polynomials Hn.

      Details and Options

      • n is a natural number with n≤80, a literal or a closed integer expression.
      • The second argument is a variable, which the polynomial is written in and which may have any name other than pi, π, Pi, e and E, or a rational number.
      • For the printed polynomial p the theorem is Polynomial.hermite n = p in ℤ[X], with p written in Polynomial.X, by CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.hermite_eq_intPoly, proved from Mathlib’s recurrence Polynomial.hermite_succ.
      • At a rational q the theorem is Polynomial.aeval q (Polynomial.hermite n) = v. With [N d] it adds the first d significant digits of v, truncated toward zero, and their decimal bounds. The report prints v = digits when the digits are exact and v ≈ digits otherwise.
      • With [N d], 1≤d≤2000, as for N.
      • #cas [expand] (HermiteHe n x) prints the same polynomial as #cas (HermiteHe n x).
      • There are no options.

      Applications (1)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and evaluate it, here at x=1:

      In[6]:=
      theorem hermiteHe_four_at_one
    (h : Polynomial.hermite 4 = Polynomial.X ^ 4 - 6 * Polynomial.X ^ 2 + 3) :
    (Polynomial.hermite 4).eval 1 = -2 := by
  rw [h]
  norm_num

#print axioms hermiteHe_four_at_one
      Out[6]=

      hermiteHe_four_at_one depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (1)

      The degree is at most 80. Mathlib’s recurrence Polynomial.hermite_succ gives higher degrees from lower ones:

      In[8]:=
      #cas (HermiteHe 81 x)
      Out[8]=

      Error

      HermiteHe accepts degrees up to 80
