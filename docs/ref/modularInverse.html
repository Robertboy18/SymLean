      Documentation › Number theory › modularInverse

      Function of #cas

      modularInverse

      The inverse of an integer modulo m≥2 in ZMod m, or a proof that there is none.

      #cas (modularInverse a m)
      gives the inverse of the integer a modulo m ≥ 2 and proves it in ZMod m, or proves that there is none.

      modularInverse finds the inverse of a modulo m, the r with ar≡1(modm). We get it from the extended Euclidean algorithm, and the theorem is about ZMod m, where the kernel checks that the product of a and the answer is 1. When a and m have a common factor there is no inverse, and the theorem you get says so instead.

      Details and Options

      • a is an integer and m a natural number with m≥2, literals or closed integer expressions.
      • When gcd(a,m)=1 the theorem is (a : ZMod m) * r = 1 ∧ (a : ZMod m)⁻¹ = r with 0≤r<m, by kernel evaluation in ZMod m and ZMod.inv_eq_of_mul_eq_one.
      • Otherwise the theorem is Nat.gcd |a| m = g ∧ ¬ IsUnit (a : ZMod m) with g>1, by ZMod.isUnit_iff_coprime, and the report prints the gcd.
      • The inverse comes from the extended Euclidean algorithm. extendedGCD prints its Bézout coefficients.
      • In a proof, cas proves (a : ZMod m)⁻¹ = b for a unit a with the certificate of the call.
      • The call is evaluated when it is the whole input of #cas.
      • There are no options.

      Basic Examples (1)

      3·4=12≡1(mod11), and the theorem has both facts:

      Applications (1)

      In a proof, cas proves the inverse with the certificate of the call:

      In[5]:=
      theorem inverse_three_mod_eleven : (3 : ZMod 11)⁻¹ = 4 := by
  cas

#print axioms inverse_three_mod_eleven
      Out[5]=

      inverse_three_mod_eleven depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Properties and Relations (1)

      extendedGCD prints the Bézout coefficients, 17·(−367)+3120·2=1. Modulo 3120 the coefficient −367 is 3120−367=2753, the inverse above:

      Out[6]=

      Possible Issues (1)

      ZMod 1 is the zero ring, where every element is its own inverse, and the call asks for m≥2:

      In[7]:=
      #cas (modularInverse 3 1)
      Out[7]=

      Error

      modularInverse: the modulus must be at least 2
