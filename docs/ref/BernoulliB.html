      Function of #cas

      BernoulliB

      Bernoulli numbers Bn and Bernoulli polynomials Bn(x) for n≤60, with theorems about Mathlib’s bernoulli and Polynomial.bernoulli.

      #cas (BernoulliB n)
      gives the Bernoulli number Bn of n ≤ 60, with B1=−12, and proves bernoulli n = q in ℚ.
      #cas (BernoulliB n x)
      gives the Bernoulli polynomial Bn in the variable x, in monomials with exact rational coefficients, and proves the expansion at every real x.
      #cas (BernoulliB n q)
      gives the exact value of Bn at the rational number q and proves it.
      #cas [N d] (BernoulliB n q)
      gives the exact value of Bn at q with its first d significant digits, truncated toward zero, and proves their decimal bounds.

      BernoulliB gives you the Bernoulli numbers and the Bernoulli polynomials. Call it with one argument and you get the number Bn. Call it with two and you get the polynomial Bn(x), whose constant term is the number Bn. The sign of B1 is a convention, and here it is Mathlib’s, B1=−12, because the theorems are about Mathlib’s bernoulli and Polynomial.bernoulli. I want the printed number to agree with the definition your proof will use.

      Details and Options

      • n is a natural number with n≤60, a literal or a closed integer expression.
      • For the number the theorem is bernoulli n = q in ℚ. Mathlib’s bernoulli' has B1′=12 instead. The proof rewrites with CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.bernoulli_eq_numerators, which holds at every n and writes Bn as entry n of the integer recurrence bernoulliNumerators n over (n+1)!, and the kernel evaluates the recurrence and decides the equation (decide +kernel).
      • B0=1, and Bn=0 for odd n≥3.
      • For the polynomial the theorem is ∀ x : ℝ, Polynomial.aeval x (Polynomial.bernoulli n) = p for the printed p, from Polynomial.coeff_bernoulli and integer Bernoulli numerators that the kernel evaluates (CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.bernoulli_eq_intPoly).
      • The second argument is a variable, which the polynomial is written in, or a rational number. At a rational q the call proves the exact value of Bn(q), and [N d] adds its first d significant digits, truncated toward zero, with their decimal bounds in the theorem. The report prints v = digits when the digits are exact and v ≈ digits otherwise.
      • #cas [expand] (BernoulliB n x) prints the same polynomial as #cas (BernoulliB n x).
      • The tactic cas does not evaluate bernoulli. A proof repeats the two steps of the command, as in the Applications below.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (2)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and specialize it, here at x=12:

      In[6]:=
      theorem bernoulliB_four_at_half
    (h : ∀ x : ℝ, Polynomial.aeval x (Polynomial.bernoulli 4) =
      x ^ 4 - 2 * x ^ 3 + x ^ 2 - 1 / 30) :
    Polynomial.aeval (1 / 2 : ℝ) (Polynomial.bernoulli 4) = 7 / 240 := by
  rw [h]
  norm_num

#print axioms bernoulliB_four_at_half
      Out[6]=

      bernoulliB_four_at_half depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      The tactic cas does not evaluate bernoulli, so a proof repeats the two steps of the command, the rewrite and the kernel’s evaluation:

      In[7]:=
      theorem bernoulli_ten : bernoulli 10 = 5 / 66 := by
  rw [CAS.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.bernoulli_eq_numerators]
  decide +kernel

#print axioms bernoulli_ten
      Out[7]=

      bernoulli_ten depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      Both forms stop at n=60, the cutoff of the command, although bernoulli_eq_numerators holds at every n and Polynomial.bernoulli_def gives the coefficients at every degree in a proof:

      In[10]:=
      #cas (BernoulliB 61)
      Out[10]=

      Error

      BernoulliB computes Bₙ for n ≤ 60
      In[11]:=
      #cas (BernoulliB 61 x)
      Out[11]=

      Error

      BernoulliB accepts degrees up to 60

      The index is an integer:

      In[12]:=
      #cas (BernoulliB (1/2))
      Out[12]=

      Error

      BernoulliB takes integer arguments; `1/2` is not an integer
