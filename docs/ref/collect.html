      Documentation › Algebra › collect

      Mode of #cas

      collect

      Writes a polynomial as a sum of powers of one variable, with coefficients in the other variables.

      #cas [collect x] e
      writes the polynomial e as a sum of powers of x whose coefficients are expanded polynomials in the other variables, and proves that the two are equal.

      A polynomial with several parameters is often easier to read once its terms are grouped by the powers of one variable, the way you would write a2x2+(2a+b)x+1 on paper. collect does that regrouping. The theorem is an identity for all real values of all the variables, proved by ring, so there are no conditions to carry around.

      Details and Options

      • The theorem is ∀x,y,…,e=∑kckxk about Mathlib’s real numbers, where the ck are the printed coefficients, proved by ring1.
      • The input is a polynomial with rational coefficients in real variables, and x is one of them. A quotient by a term with a variable is refused.
      • The terms are printed from the highest power of x down, and a coefficient that is a sum is put in parentheses.
      • coefficient gives one coefficient and coefficientList all of them, with theorems about Mathlib’s Polynomial.coeff. exponent gives the degree.
      • The tactic cas proves these identities.

      Applications (1)

      The first example as a theorem, for all real x, a and b. Only the three standard axioms are used:

      In[4]:=
      theorem collect_in_x (x a b : ℝ) :
    (1 + a * x) ^ 2 + b * x = a ^ 2 * x ^ 2 + (2 * a + b) * x + 1 := by
  cas

#print axioms collect_in_x
      Out[4]=

      collect_in_x depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      A quotient by a term with a variable is refused:

      In[10]:=
      #cas [collect x] (1/x + x)
      Out[10]=

      Error

      collect: the expression is not a polynomial (it divides by a term with a variable)

      For a rational function, apart splits off the polynomial part:

      In[11]:=
      #cas [apart] (x^3/(x^2+1))
      Out[11]=

      partial fractions over the written factors, refined by gcds

      x−xx2+1
      x - x/(x^2 + 1)
      Input (rational function)
      (x^3/(x^2+1))
      Conditions
