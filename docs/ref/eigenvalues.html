      Documentation › Linear algebra › eigenvalues

      Mode of #cas

      eigenvalues

      The exact eigenvalues of a square matrix with rational entries, with their multiplicities and a theorem that there are no others.

      #cas [eigenvalues] A
      gives the eigenvalues of the square matrix A with rational entries, each as often as its algebraic multiplicity, and proves that there are no others.

      eigenvalues gives you the eigenvalues exactly. They come back as rational numbers, quadratic surds such as 12+52, complex numbers, or roots of a factor of higher degree, and a root of that last kind is named by an interval that contains it. I reach for this mode when the eigenvalues have to go into a proof, because the theorem doesn’t stop at saying that each number is an eigenvalue. It says the list is the whole spectrum. When every eigenvalue is rational, that theorem is a factorization of the characteristic polynomial over ℚ. Otherwise it is a statement about Mathlib’s spectrum over ℝ or ℂ, together with the roots of A.charpoly and their multiplicities.

      Details and Options

      • A is a square matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]]. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • When every eigenvalue is rational, the theorem is A.charpoly = (X - λ₁) ^ m₁ * ⋯ * (X - λₖ) ^ mₖ in ℚ[X].
      • Otherwise let M be the matrix with the entries of A over K=ℝ when every eigenvalue is real, and over K=ℂ when one is not. The theorem is spectrum K M = {μ₁, …, μₛ}, each eigenvalue once, and M.charpoly.roots = {μ₁, …}, the multiset with the multiplicities.
      • A root of a factor R of degree 3 or more is named in the theorem by let r₁ := CAS.AlgRoot.real R lo hi, with r₁ ∈ Set.Ioo lo hi and R(r1)=0, or by CAS.AlgRoot.complex R u v ρ, with intervals for its real and imaginary parts. The report prints the intervals.
      • The kernel checks that the factors multiply to the characteristic polynomial (CAS.Analysis.Matrix.eigenCheck).
      • Without Hex, a factor of higher degree comes from the squarefree decomposition of the characteristic polynomial and need not be irreducible, and each of its roots gets its own interval. With the optional Hex library (import CASHex.Factor), the factors are irreducible: the theorem adds Irreducible R for each factor R whose irreducibility Hex certifies, and the report lists the others.
      • There is no limit on the size of A. The search for the eigenvalues runs before the proof, and the proof runs with maxHeartbeats raised to at least one million, so a large matrix can take long.

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and read an eigenvalue off it, here the golden ratio:

      In[9]:=
      theorem golden_ratio_mem_spectrum
    (h : spectrum ℝ !![(0 : ℝ), 1; 1, 1] = {1 / 2 - 1 / 2 * √5, 1 / 2 + 1 / 2 * √5}) :
    1 / 2 + 1 / 2 * √5 ∈ spectrum ℝ !![(0 : ℝ), 1; 1, 1] := by
  rw [h]
  exact Set.mem_insert_of_mem _ (Set.mem_singleton _)
