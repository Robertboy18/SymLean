      Documentation › Differential equations › fourier

      Mode of #cas

      fourier

      The Fourier coefficients of a function on [-π, π] as exact functions of n, with a theorem about the integrals.

      #cas [fourier x n] f
      gives the Fourier coefficients a0, an and bn of f on [−π,π] as exact functions of n for n≥1, and proves the integrals.

      fourier computes the Fourier coefficients of a concrete function and proves their integral formulas. The theorem is about Mathlib’s interval integrals over [−π,π], with one statement for a0 and one each for an and bn that holds at every n≥1. So you get the coefficients as functions of n, not a table of the first few.

      The method is the one you would use by hand. The function is split into terms cxjeaxcoskx and cxjeaxsinkx, and each one is integrated by parts. The case I always have to be careful with by hand is resonance. When a term has a frequency k≥1 and no exponential, the coefficients at n=k differ from the general formula, and the report gives them by an if instead of leaving a formula that divides by zero there.

      Details and Options

      • For the printed coefficients, the theorem is 1 / Real.pi * ∫ x in -Real.pi..Real.pi, f = a₀ and, for every n : ℕ with 0 < n, 1 / Real.pi * ∫ x in -Real.pi..Real.pi, f * Real.cos (n * x) = aₙ and the same with Real.sin and bₙ. They are the coefficients of the series a0/2+∑n≥1(ancosnx+bnsinnx).
      • f is a sum of products of polynomials in x, exponentials exp (a * x) with rational a, and sines and cosines with integer frequencies, such as x * sin x, x ^ 2 * exp x or sin x ^ 3 * cos (2 * x).
      • When a term c * x ^ j * cos (k * x) or c * x ^ j * sin (k * x) has k ≥ 1, the coefficients at n = k are given by an if, unless the formula for general n covers them.
      • When the proof of the coefficients fails, for instance at the heartbeat limit, the command is refused.
      • There are no options. In a proof, cas proves a single coefficient, as under Applications.

      Possible Issues (2)

      Other functions are refused. nintegrate encloses a single coefficient of them numerically:

      In[12]:=
      #cas [fourier x n] (exp (x ^ 2))
      Out[12]=

      Error

      fourier: the function must be a sum of products of polynomials, exponentials, sines and cosines

      On [−π,π] the frequencies are integers, as in sin (k * x):

      In[13]:=
      #cas [fourier x n] (sin (x / 2))
      Out[13]=

      Error

      fourier: trigonometric frequencies must be integers on [-π, π]
