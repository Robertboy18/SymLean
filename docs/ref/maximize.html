      Documentation › Calculus › maximize

      Mode of #cas

      maximize

      The maximum of a polynomial on the real line or on an interval, exact or enclosed, with a theorem.

      #cas [maximize x] p
      gives the global maximum of the polynomial p, exactly when a rational point attains it and otherwise as a certified enclosure, and proves that no value of p is larger.
      #cas [maximize x in [a, b]] p
      gives the maximum of p on the closed interval [a,b] with rational endpoints a<b, with a point of the interval that attains it or comes within the enclosure.
      #cas (digits := d) [maximize x] p
      the same with an enclosure of width at most 2·10−d, for d from 1 to 6. The interval form takes d too.

      maximize looks for the largest value of a polynomial and proves that nothing goes above it. It is minimize applied to −p, with the inequalities turned around, so everything on that page holds here too.

      You get one of two answers. When a rational point attains the maximum, the answer is exact, and the theorem says p(x)≤M for every real x, and p(x0)=M. Otherwise the answer is an enclosure, and the theorem says p(x)≤hi for every x, and lo≤p(x1) at a rational point x1 near the maximizer.

      Details and Options

      • p is a polynomial in x with rational coefficients and no other variable.
      • The exact theorem is (∀x,p≤M)∧p(x0)=M, and the enclosure theorem is (∀x,p≤hi)∧lo≤p(x1). Lean writes the value at the point as (fun x ↦ p) x₀.
      • A polynomial of odd degree, or of even degree with a positive leading coefficient, is unbounded above and is refused. On a closed interval every polynomial has a maximum.
      • On [a,b] the bound is ∀x∈[a,b],p≤M, stated with Set.Icc a b, and the point is proved to lie in the interval.
      • (digits := d) makes the enclosure at most 2·10−d wide, for d from 1 to 6, by default 3.
      • In a proof, cas proves the bound ∀x,p≤M, and cega proves bounds on an interval, also for functions other than polynomials.

      Applications (2)

      The bound in a proof. cas proves it, and only the three standard axioms are used:

      In[9]:=
      theorem maximize_quadratic : ∀ x : ℝ, 3 + 2 * x - x ^ 2 ≤ 4 := by
  cas

#print axioms maximize_quadratic
      Out[9]=

      maximize_quadratic depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      A polynomial of even degree with a positive leading coefficient has no maximum:

      In[13]:=
      #cas [maximize x] (x^2)
      Out[13]=

      Error

      maximize: the polynomial is unbounded above (degree 2, leading coefficient 1), so it has no maximum
