      Documentation › #cas

      Command

      #cas

      The calculator: evaluates a call, simplifies an expression or runs a mode, and proves its answer.

      #cas e
      evaluates e when it is a call of a special or number-theoretic function, such as gamma (5/2), and otherwise simplifies it as #cas [simplify] does.
      #cas [mode] e
      runs a mode on e, such as derive x, factor or solve x.
      #cas (options) [mode] e
      the same, with the options of the mode, such as (digits := 30).

      #cas is the calculator, and for me it is where most work with SymLean starts. You type an expression and get the answer, together with the theorem behind it, checked by Lean’s kernel. Nothing needs setting up beyond import CAS, and names like x and t are variables, as they would be on paper.

      Details and Options

      • A report gives the output and its kind, the domain of the variables and the conditions under which the output holds, such as x2−1≠0.
      • The details of a report, in the InfoView, hold the computation and the checked steps. Each step is a proposition, the theorem that proves it, and the check that it passed.
      • #cas e evaluates e when it is a call of ChebyshevT, ChebyshevU, HermiteH, HermiteHe, LegendreP, BernoulliB, zeta, gamma, eulerPhi, divisorSigma, binomial, factorial, fibonacci, powerMod, modularInverse or jacobiSymbol, and of primeQ and moebiusMu with import CASHex.NumberTheory. erf and BesselJ have digits only, by #cas [N d].
      • The call is the whole input, up to parentheses. In #cas (2 * gamma (5/2)) nothing is evaluated.
      • Integer arguments are closed integer expressions such as 2 ^ 10 - 1, and rational arguments closed rational expressions such as 5/2. A call is refused when an argument or its value needs a number with more than 10000 digits.
      • For any other input, the theorem is that of #cas [simplify] e: ∀x1,…,xn, c1→⋯→ck→e=e′, with the conditions ci of the report.
      • A bracket right after #cas always opens a mode. A word that is no mode is refused, and the message names the nearest modes, or the commands for a function of the table.
      • The modes are listed by area in the guides: Algebra, Polynomials, Calculus, Series and Limits, Equations, Sums and Recurrences, Number Theory, Linear Algebra, Differential Equations and Numerics.
      • #cas e has no options. The settings that shape a report are on Options.

      Possible Issues (6)

      The call must be the whole input:

      In[15]:=
      #cas (2 * gamma (5/2))
      Out[15]=

      Error

      unsupported symbolic function 'gamma'; expected abs, sin, cos, tan, sinh, cosh, tanh, atan/arctan, asin/arcsin, acos/arccos, exp, log, or sqrt. `gamma x` is evaluated as the whole input of `#cas (gamma x)`

      A family of polynomials needs the variable too:

      In[16]:=
      #cas (ChebyshevT 5)
      Out[16]=

      Error

      ChebyshevT takes 2 arguments, as in `ChebyshevT n x`; the call has 1

      The error function has no exact values, only digits:

      In[17]:=
      #cas (erf 1)
      Out[17]=

      Error

      `#cas` computes no exact value of `erf x`; `#cas [N d] (erf x)` gives its digits

      Mode words are case sensitive:

      In[18]:=
      #cas [Simplify] (x + x)
      Out[18]=

      Error

      `Simplify` is no mode of `#cas`; the nearest mode is `simplify`

      A function in the bracket gets the commands that evaluate it:

      In[19]:=
      #cas [LegendreP 5]
      Out[19]=

      Error

      `LegendreP` is no mode of `#cas`; write `#cas (LegendreP n x)`, `#cas [expand] (LegendreP n x)` or `#cas [N d] (LegendreP n x)`

      A value with more than 10000 digits is refused:

      In[20]:=
      #cas (factorial 5000)
      Out[20]=

      Error

      factorial: the value has more than 10000 digits, the most that a call computes
