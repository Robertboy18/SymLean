      Documentation › Number theory › extendedGCD

      Mode of #cas

      extendedGCD

      The gcd of two natural numbers with Bézout coefficients s and t, and a theorem that as+bt=gcd(a,b).

      #cas [extendedGCD] (a, b)
      gives the gcd g of the natural numbers a and b with integers s and t such that a s + b t = g, and proves both facts.

      Whenever I need to invert a number modulo another, solve a linear equation ax+by=c in integers, or merge two congruences, what I really need is a pair of Bézout coefficients. extendedGCD runs the extended Euclidean algorithm and prints the answer the way Mathematica’s ExtendedGCD does, as g, [s, t]. The theorem carries the two facts I end up using: g is Mathlib’s Nat.gcd a b, and as+bt=g in ℤ.

      Details and Options

      • a and b are natural numbers: literals, or closed integer expressions such as 2^32. The coefficients s and t are integers.
      • The theorem is Nat.gcd a b = g ∧ (a : ℤ) * s + b * t = g. The kernel evaluates the gcd (decide +kernel), and norm_num checks the equation.
      • The coefficients are those of the extended Euclidean algorithm.
      • There are no options.

      Applications (3)

      The theorem, with the axioms its proof uses:

      In[5]:=
      theorem bezout_240_46 : Nat.gcd 240 46 = 2 ∧ (240 : ℤ) * (-9) + 46 * 47 = 2 := by
  cas

#print axioms bezout_240_46
      Out[5]=

      bezout_240_46 depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      Possible Issues (2)

      The arguments are natural numbers. Since gcd(−a,b)=gcd(a,b), the coefficients for −a are those for a with the sign of s reversed:

      In[11]:=
      #cas [extendedGCD] (-240, 46)
      Out[11]=

      Error

      extendedGCD: expected a natural number

      The mode takes exactly two numbers:

      In[12]:=
      #cas [extendedGCD] (12, 18, 30)
      Out[12]=

      Error

      extendedGCD: expected `(a, b)`
