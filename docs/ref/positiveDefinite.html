      Documentation › Linear algebra › positiveDefinite

      Mode of #cas

      positiveDefinite

      Whether a square matrix with rational entries is positive definite, with a theorem about Mathlib’s Matrix.PosDef either way.

      #cas [positiveDefinite] A
      decides whether the square matrix A with rational entries is positive definite over ℝ, and proves the answer.

      For a symmetric matrix, positive definiteness means xTAx>0 for every nonzero x. It is the condition behind the second-derivative test and many quadratic-form estimates. positiveDefinite proves its answer either way, and the two proofs are of quite different kinds. True comes with an exact factorization A=LDLT over ℚ with positive pivots. False comes with a rational vector x≠0 such that xTAx≤0, which you can check by hand, or with an entry that breaks the symmetry.

      Details and Options

      • A is a square matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]]. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • The theorem is M.PosDef or ¬M.PosDef for the matrix M : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ with the entries of A. Mathlib’s Matrix.PosDef asks that M be Hermitian, which over ℝ means symmetric, and that xTMx>0 for every x≠0.
      • True comes from the symmetric elimination A=LDLT over ℚ, with all pivots in D positive. The report prints the pivots, and the kernel checks the factorization entry by entry and the signs of the pivots (CAS.Analysis.Matrix.posDef_of_ldlCheck).
      • False names a rational vector x≠0 with xTAx≤0, and the kernel checks its quadratic form (CAS.Analysis.Matrix.not_posDef_of_quadFormCheck). For a matrix that is not symmetric, the theorem comes from one entry with Aij≠Aji.
      • The elimination is exact, so there is no tolerance: a pivot of 10−15 counts as positive.

      Applications (2)

      A proof can take the printed theorem as a hypothesis and use Mathlib’s consequences of positive definiteness, here Matrix.PosDef.det_pos:

      In[7]:=
      theorem posDef_det_pos (h : (!![2, 1; 1, 2] : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ).PosDef) :
    0 < (!![2, 1; 1, 2] : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ).det :=
  h.det_pos

#print axioms posDef_det_pos
      Out[7]=

      posDef_det_pos depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
