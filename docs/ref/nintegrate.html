      Documentation › Numerics › nintegrate

      Mode of #cas

      nintegrate

      A definite integral to a fixed number of decimal places, with a theorem that bounds the error.

      #cas [nintegrate x in [a, b]] f
      gives the integral of f from a to b as a decimal c with 6 places, and proves |∫abfdx−c|≤10−6.
      #cas (digits := d) [nintegrate x in [a, b]] f
      the same with d decimal places, from 1 to 12.

      Some integrands have no elementary antiderivative, e−x2 among them, and even when there is one, I often find a decimal with an error bound more useful than the closed formula. That is what nintegrate gives you: a decimal, and a theorem about Mathlib’s interval integral that says how far the decimal can be from the true value.

      Details and Options

      • For the printed c±10−d the theorem is |(∫abfdx)−c|≤1/10d, stated with ∫ x in a..b.
      • The proof is a composite Simpson rule. Its remainder comes from an enclosure of the fourth derivative of f on every panel, and the number of panels doubles from 4 to 1024 until the decimals are fixed.
      • A domain checker proves that f and its first four derivatives are defined on [a,b]. A factor x or a fractional power of x at 0 is first removed by a substitution, so ∫01xdx works.
      • The limits are closed rational expressions with a<b, such as -1 or 3/2. f is an expression in x alone, in the grammar of N.
      • (digits := d) sets the number of decimal places, from 1 to 12, by default 6.
      • The tactic cega proves the printed statement in a proof, and also bounds such as ∫abf≤c for integrands that are only continuous.

      Applications (2)

      The printed value in a proof. cega proves the same statement, and only the three standard axioms are used:

      In[10]:=
      theorem nintegrate_gauss :
    |(∫ x in (0 : ℝ)..1, Real.exp (-x ^ 2)) - 746824 / 10 ^ 6| ≤ 1 / 10 ^ 6 := by
  cega

#print axioms nintegrate_gauss
      Out[10]=

      nintegrate_gauss depends on the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

      A one-sided bound is often all a proof needs:

      In[11]:=
      example : (∫ x in (0 : ℝ)..1, Real.exp (-x ^ 2)) ≤ 3 / 4 := by
  cega
      Out[11]=

      ✓ Lean accepts the proof.

      Possible Issues (3)

      A pole in the interval. 1/x has no finite integral on [0,1], and no certificate exists:

      In[16]:=
      #cas [nintegrate x in [0, 1]] (1 / x)
      Out[16]=

      Error

      nintegrate: the domain checker found no fourth-derivative validity certificate for the integrand on [0, 1] and no substitution removes the singularity; a bound such as `(∫ x in a..b, f x) ≤ c` is proved by `cega`, whose range panels need only continuity

      At most 12 places:

      In[17]:=
      #cas (digits := 16) [nintegrate x in [0, 1]] (x)
      Out[17]=

      Error

      nintegrate accepts between 1 and 12 digits

      The smaller limit comes first:

      In[18]:=
      #cas [nintegrate x in [1, 0]] (x)
      Out[18]=

      Error

      nintegrate needs rational limits a < b
