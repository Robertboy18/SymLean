      Documentation › Linear algebra › nullSpace

      Mode of #cas

      nullSpace

      A basis of the null space of a matrix with rational entries, with a theorem that it spans every solution.

      #cas [nullSpace] A
      gives a basis of the null space of the matrix A with rational entries, the vectors x with Ax=0, and proves that it is a basis.

      nullSpace gives a basis for the solutions of Ax=0, and applied to A−λI it gives an eigenspace. The theorem says three things about the printed vectors Vi, over ℚ: each one solves Ax=0, they are linearly independent, and every solution is a combination of them. You could check the first by multiplying. The other two are what make the list a basis and not only a list of solutions, and they are the ones I want a proof of.

      Details and Options

      • A is a matrix literal !![a, b; c, d] or a list of rows [[a, b], [c, d]] of any shape. The entries are closed rational expressions such as -3/2, 0.25 or 1/10^15.
      • The theorem is A.mulVec (V i) = 0 for each i, LinearIndependent ℚ V, and ∀ x, A.mulVec x = 0 → ∃ c, x = ∑ i, c i • V i. When only the zero vector solves the equation, the output is [] (only the zero vector) and the theorem is ∀ x, A.mulVec x = 0 → x = 0.
      • The vectors come from the free columns of the reduced row echelon form: each has a 1 at its own free column and 0 at the other free ones. The printed dimension is the number of columns minus the rank.
      • linarith proves the statement about every x from the coordinates of A.mulVec x, and the kernel evaluates the rest.
      • The tactic cas proves goals A.mulVec v = 0 for literals over ℚ.

      Possible Issues (1)

      The mode takes the matrix alone. For a right side, use linearSolve:

      Out[12]=

      Error

      expected a matrix `!![a, b; c, d]` or `[[a, b], [c, d]]`
